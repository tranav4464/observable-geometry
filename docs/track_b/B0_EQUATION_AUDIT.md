## Candidate object and necessary distinctions

For source hypotheses `h_i,h_j`, scenario `m`, admissible nuisance parameters `theta_i,theta_j`, and noise covariance `C_m`, the working score is a constrained, whitened separation between predicted sensor measurements. The robust variant aggregates scenario-specific scores or minimizes over a prespecified family. A threshold would produce **pairwise ambiguity decisions**, not automatically a partition: indistinguishability need not be transitive. An empirical link from predicted separation to held-out site-discrimination error is a separate statistical model, not a theorem following from the distance alone.

This object can only be a research contribution if four choices are pinned and tested: physical hypothesis family, nuisance constraints, uncertainty-set construction, and an operating-point decision calibrated across participants. None of those ingredients alone is claimed to be new.

## Collision verdict and kill rule

The broad idea “cluster sources EEG cannot distinguish” is **not novel**. Kalogianni et al. already operationalized EEG spatial resolution through simulated two-source separability under realistic noise, distance and temporal-correlation variation. The working fixed-model pair score is also exactly the classical Mahalanobis/ideal-observer discriminability for shared-covariance Gaussian means; its Gaussian error mapping is standard. Source ambiguity, robust testing over uncertainty sets, “show alternative sources,” “bound spatial resolution,” and broad scalp-to-intracranial recoverability taxonomies are established or actively proposed. Therefore the candidate's remaining possible contribution is not currently mathematical novelty: it is the unproven combination of a physically defensible task-specific forward uncertainty set with an empirical, out-of-participant prediction of actual pairwise experimental confusion. The 2026 virtual-iEEG review makes a generic recoverability framework especially difficult to defend as novel. Any surviving contribution must be an exact operational algorithm and independently useful result beyond separability, the review, prior art and direct reconstruction models. It may still be an incremental benchmark/application rather than a new framework. B0 must explicitly decide this after matched implementation; if no exact differentiator and benefit remain, relabel the work as a rigorous replication/benchmark or pivot to a different obstacle. No breakthrough claim is justified by the present evidence.

The shared-covariance Gaussian baseline is now implemented in `src/observable_geometry/track_b/classical_baselines.py`. It uses the covariance-supported subspace, explicitly rejects means with unsupported components (including reference/rank mistakes), and returns the equal-prior, single-observation Bayes-error value only under its stated assumptions. Its closed-form and rank-deficient behavior are covered by `tests/test_track_b_classical_baselines.py`; this is comparator infrastructure, not a Track B invention.
