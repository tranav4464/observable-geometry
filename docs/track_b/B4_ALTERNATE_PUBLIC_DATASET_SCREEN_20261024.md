**Screen date:** 24 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Determine whether an already public real dataset can reopen B4 for EEG source-pattern distinguishability under an individual forward model.  
**Data boundary:** Metadata and published descriptions only. No neural-signal samples were opened for this screen.

## Decision

**No screened alternative presently reopens B4–B6 as an independent confirmatory evaluation.** One important candidate, the public iES-CCEP dataset, has participant anatomy and stimulation-site information for source-imaging work. However, the 2026 GBF paper already reports localization results for 318 sessions from 35 participants from this data route. The OSF API assigns the project an “Other” license and points to `license.txt`, but a recursive metadata-only inventory lists seven visible files and no such file. The EBRAINS mirror is discoverable, but its public page response exposed only an app shell and its instance API returned HTTP 401; dataset-specific reuse terms therefore remain unresolved through both routes. It could support a carefully labelled secondary analysis of a different frozen estimand, but not independent confirmation of GBF localization. This is a feasibility and prior-art decision, not evidence that no other qualifying dataset exists. See `B4_OSF_WSGZP_LICENSE_AUDIT_20261024.md`.

## Requirements for a qualifying dataset

To test whether the current GBF distinguishability output predicts a useful real-data outcome, the dataset must provide, for the same participant and acquisition:

1. scalp EEG with sensor positions, channel names/order, units, reference, and preprocessing provenance;
2. a participant-specific forward model or the MRI, tissue surfaces, transforms, electrode positions, and conductivity information needed to recreate and verify one;
3. an independent, spatially registered reference appropriate to the outcome, such as intracranial contacts and event timing, while acknowledging that SEEG samples only implanted locations;
4. enough independently repeated events/conditions and participants for the proposed split and unit of inference;
5. public access, license, and data-use terms that permit the proposed reproducible analysis; and
6. a task-level prior-art comparison showing what new decision the GBF output would add beyond existing ESI, resolution, source-separability, and EEG–SEEG comparison methods.

Availability of scalp EEG and intracranial EEG somewhere in a cohort is not sufficient: the pairing, geometry, reference, independent unit, and intended outcome must all be verified.

For a clinical source-localization outcome, a paired intracranial recording is not the only possible reference. A prespecified comparison to a resection cavity and postsurgical outcome can be informative, but it is an indirect clinical proxy for the epileptogenic zone, not direct observation of the complete source. Such a route needs patient-level separation, explicit definitions of concordance, and careful treatment of outcome and clinical-selection limitations.

## Prior-art implications

The alternatives reinforce rather than remove the novelty constraint. EEG–SEEG translation and source imaging already have direct literature, including the 2026 GBF paper's own 318-session stimulation-site localization evaluation. Barborica et al. also report simultaneous scalp–SEEG seizure-onset analysis using ICA, dipole localization, and intracranial comparisons, but use an MNI-template head model because scalp electrode positions were not digitized. EBRAINS indexes a versioned `v1` dataset record for this work; the exact files and data-use terms remain unverified. This is close prior art and a possible separate reproduction route, not a participant-specific validation route for B2. The iES-CCEP archive is a promising geometry-rich resource, but its broad GBF localization result is already public. A secondary question about incremental predictive value of the frozen B2 score could be distinct from that paper's inverse-method comparison, but the result would be post-publication and the OSF API’s `Other` license points to a `license.txt` absent from the visible storage inventory, leaving reuse terms unresolved. A dataset alone would not make the GBF cosine a new method. A catalog-wide NEMAR metadata query found four records carrying both exact modality labels; `nm000434` has paired scalp/LFP data but lacks the active geometry contract, while `on001785` combines separate EEG and ECoG cohorts. Any reopening needs a distinct, testable decision, matched established comparators, clear data-use terms, and careful treatment of the stimulation site as a reference for the evoked measurement rather than complete ground truth for all neural sources. See `B4_NEMAR_CATALOG_SCREEN_20261024.md` for the query and bounded findings.

The HDEEG-IED-SurgOutcome release is a candidate with participant MRI, electrode coordinates, EEG averages, postsurgical imaging, and outcome labels. Published counts imply a 20-person remainder outside the 24-person GBF resection analysis, but it is from the same cohort, not an external cohort; exact participant identities and QC eligibility remain unknown. The six favourable remainder cases may have been excluded by quality control, and the 14 unfavourable cases do not supply straightforward resection-based source truth. The EEG is averaged and heterogeneous, so compatibility with the frozen B2 covariance contract also remains unresolved. Any separately reviewed route would need an outcome-blind crosswalk, verified data-use terms, a prespecified and scientifically defensible estimand, and a locked protocol before access. See `B4_HDEEG_PUBLISHED_OVERLAP_ADDENDUM_20261024.md`.

The Unnwongse et al. 2023 study is a separate, technically relevant candidate for future collaboration: it reports simultaneous stimulation-evoked scalp EEG and stereo-EEG, individual FEM models, and a known stimulation-contact reference. Its paper states that data are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request, so the publication does not provide an immediately reproducible public-data route. It may be worth revisiting if the project gains an authorized collaboration; no author has been contacted and no data have been requested or accessed.

The 2025 Hao et al. study is also close to the broader empirical direction: it reports simultaneous 64-channel scalp EEG and SEEG, uses SEEG-informed source imaging, and evaluates localization distance. Its associated DRE-LZSH data are now publicly available through Zenodo and NEMAR, correcting the earlier screen that no public DOI had been identified. The release is described as an SEEG benchmark; its README reports scalp-labelled channels mixed into the recordings, but no electrode coordinates, unspecified units, and no event timing. The NEMAR participant table reports MRI status codes rather than released participant MRI. It may support a separate signal-prediction task, but it does not satisfy the individual-geometry contract for the frozen GBF source-distinguishability estimand.

## Gate consequences

- **B4:** Remains **PIVOT** for independent empirical validation of endogenous source distinguishability. The Localize-MI artifact branch remains closed to confirmatory outcomes. The iES-CCEP archive may support a secondary analysis of a distinct score-to-error estimand, but the 2026 GBF paper already analyzes nearly all of its sessions for localization. Its OSF license record points to a `license.txt` absent from the visible storage inventory; the EBRAINS mirror requires authenticated metadata access, and its terms were not available through the public app shell. Data-use terms therefore remain unresolved through both routes before signal access. See `B4_OSF_WSGZP_LICENSE_AUDIT_20261024.md` and its metadata-only audit script/output. This screen does not authorize signal access.
- **B5:** Remains **PIVOT**. The HDEEG-IED-SurgOutcome release is a plausible secondary-evaluation candidate, but its EBRAINS terms, overlap with the published GBF subset, precise score-to-outcome estimand, and independent protocol review have not been resolved. No outcome access is authorized by this metadata screen.
- **B6:** Remains **PIVOT**. No held-out outcome analysis was performed for these candidates.
- **B7:** Remains **PIVOT** for researcher decision utility. Model-only association with MNE PSF summaries is descriptive and cannot establish decision benefit.
- **B8:** This screen is additional provenance for the release packet. It does not replace the outstanding unaffiliated external review or independent reproduction.

## Reopening checklist

Reopen the empirical branch only after a candidate has a versioned public data record and license, a metadata-only pairing/geometry audit, adequate independent units for a prespecified split, a precise outcome and decision, a matched prior-art review, and independent technical review of a frozen protocol. If no candidate meets these conditions, finish the bounded reproduction and state plainly that no valid empirical test or novel method was established.
