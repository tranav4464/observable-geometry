# B0: GBFs extension and contribution screen

**Status: literature re-audit, 24 October 2026.** This is a route and claim-boundary analysis, not an independent peer review or novelty certification. It adds primary-source checks of major 2026 developments to the earlier screen. It does not inspect new participant signals, fit models, or generate synthetic data.

## Decision this screen informs

Does the existing nominal source-signature calculation establish a distinct mathematical method, and is a forward-model ensemble alone a defensible extension?

**Finding:** neither is established. The current score is a useful, inspectable calculation, but pairwise source separability, resolution-matrix evaluation, and forward-model uncertainty have substantial prior art. A software contribution can still be worthwhile if it makes a researcher decision reproducible and auditable. Any distinct scientific contribution requires a precisely stated decision and evidence that the software changes it beyond established comparators.

## What the current score measures

For a fixed leadfield (L), sensor-noise covariance (C), and source columns (g_i,g_j), Track A forms noise-whitened, unit-norm signatures

\[
u_i = \frac{C^{-1/2}g_i}{\lVert C^{-1/2}g_i\rVert}, \qquad
\rho_{ij}=|u_i^\top u_j|.
\]

The absolute value treats polarity as a nuisance and unit normalization treats source amplitude as a nuisance. A large \(\rho_{ij}\) means the two focal-source hypotheses have similar predicted sensor topographies under the declared model. The quantity \(\sqrt{1-\rho_{ij}^2}\) is the residual norm after projecting one whitened, unit-norm signature onto the other. It is not, by itself, a probability of correct localization or a calibrated error rate. Such an interpretation needs an explicit observation model, signal scale, hypothesis definition, and empirical calibration.

This is a **nominal pairwise geometry diagnostic**. It does not test simultaneous source recovery, arbitrary distributed sources, model uncertainty, or biological source truth. The existing Track A pulse-artifact experiment is a separate empirical task with known stimulation contacts; its results do not calibrate the pairwise score without a prespecified, adequately supported mapping from score to observed discrimination.

## Candidate contribution and kill criteria

1. **Retain as the core software build:** a clean, documented GBFs reproduction plus an inspectable real-data workflow that validates the forward-model contract, computes nominal pair scores, compares them with standard resolution diagnostics, and records provenance and claim limits. This is the user's stated goal and can be delivered without claiming a new algorithm.
2. **Do not label the current cosine score a new method.** Its broad mathematical ingredients and source-separability interpretation overlap with established work. Track A parity supports implementation agreement on the selected inputs, not novelty or full paper-level GBFs reproduction.
3. **Treat a forward-model ensemble as a hypothesis only.** It becomes a candidate extension only if the allowed ensemble is based on documented, physically meaningful uncertainty for the selected real participant, produces an output that differs from established global sensitivity, Bayesian/model-averaging, posterior-ambiguity, and source-separability reports, and changes a prespecified source-reporting decision in a real-data evaluation. A set of arbitrary perturbed leadfields or a better-looking visualization fails this bar. The May 2026 information-theoretic bounds and ambiguity-decomposition preprint, and the September 2026 multilayer MEG paper, strengthen this kill criterion.
4. **Evaluate practical value before expanding scope.** Use the existing permitted Localize-MI development evidence only after the question, score-to-decision mapping, and comparisons are specified. The six-site, one-participant Track A results are exploratory and cannot support population calibration. No additional data outcomes or synthetic neural data should be used to make the case look stronger.
5. **Do not reopen the proposed artifact-only held-out test on metadata feasibility alone.** The multi-participant metadata audit found one run per site/intensity condition. A within-run trial split cannot establish run-independent transfer. A whole-run cross-intensity split leaves only a small and uneven number of geometry-eligible site-pair comparisons among the six untouched participants (1, 1, 0, 12, 2, and 3 respectively); this is insufficient for the originally proposed participant-level ranking and top-quartile decision analysis. See `B4_ARTIFACT_VALIDATION_REOPENING_PROPOSAL_20261024.md` and its metadata report. A different independently validated dataset or estimand is required before reconsidering confirmatory B5/B6 work.

**Falsification / stop rule:** if the candidate output is reducible to a standard pairwise separability score, resolution-matrix summary, or ordinary forward-model sensitivity report and does not change a predeclared researcher decision on the permitted real evidence, stop the new-method branch. Ship the faithful reproduction and auditable tool as a rigorous engineering/benchmark build, and state plainly that no distinct method survived.

## Primary-source evidence checked

- Kalogianni et al. 2018, publisher record/abstract and DOI. The paper directly studies two-source separability under noise and source-distance variation.
- Pascual-Marqui & Biscay-Lirio 1993, DOI record and publisher metadata. The paper explicitly introduces source-resolution information functionals, including an angle-based comparison between source measurement patterns. This is a close conceptual predecessor to the current normalized-cosine diagnostic.
- Hauk et al. 2022, full author manuscript. The manuscript defines and applies PSF/CTF and resolution-matrix metrics, explains dataset-specific evaluation, and notes the limitations of EEG/MEG spatial inference.
- Scherg et al. 2022, [primary full-text article](https://pure.au.dk/ws/files/339865547/1-s2.0-S1388245721007719-main.pdf). Appendix A explicitly normalizes leadfield vectors and forms the cross-correlation matrix `LₙᵀLₙ`; RSP then uses a probe-plus-shield inverse and is evaluated against clinical references. This is close mathematical prior art for normalized leadfield-column comparisons, although its output and task differ from the project's pairwise forward diagnostic.
- Stenroos & Hauk 2009 and Stenroos et al. 2010, DTU repository records. These directly establish prior work on EEG forward-model errors and hierarchical forward-model uncertainty.
- Vorwerk, Wolters & Baumgarten 2024, the [primary open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10963400/). It directly evaluates conductivity uncertainty across 1,000 parameter sets and maps effects on forward topography and source localization, including Sobol sensitivity. This rules out treating a conductivity ensemble or sensitivity map alone as novel.
- Barnes, Guo, Dytso & Grover 2026, the [AISTATS/PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/barnes26a.html). The primary paper establishes asymptotic minimax localization-risk bounds for its stated point-source/sensor model and includes a simulated realistic-head EEG comparison. It addresses fundamental single-source localization limits, not the exact pairwise estimand in B2.
- Guo, Deng & Grover 2026, [arXiv:2605.15050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15050). The preprint introduces a cascade decomposition for intrinsic ambiguity versus estimation uncertainty, and applies calibration diagnostics to EEG source imaging. Its use of learned deep generative models differs from the B2 deterministic signature calculation, but narrows any defensible claim about novelty in ambiguity accounting.
- Szul et al. 2026, [Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77782-9). This paper directly uses lead-field separability to analyze depth-resolved inference and evaluates anatomical and acquisition constraints in a multilayer hpMEG framework. It further rules out a generic claim that finer non-invasive mapping via separability analysis is itself a new direction.
- Qin, Tong & Sun 2026, [IEEE TNSRE](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNSRE.2026.3688271) and [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42048205/). This is a close study using the same Localize-MI seven-participant dataset, comparing eight inverse methods across electrode-quality scenarios. Its endpoint is localization error/spatial dispersion, not the proposed forward-score prediction of cross-intensity pattern discrimination; the overlap is unresolved and must be stated explicitly in any future comparison.
- Unnwongse et al. 2026, [Epilepsy Research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eplepsyres.2026.107764) and [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41764909/). This adjacent study uses stimulation-derived region-specific error vectors to improve ESI localization of both evoked responses and interictal discharges. The abstract does not establish cohort identity with Localize-MI; it is relevant prior art for empirical stimulation-based localization calibration, not evidence about the present dataset.
- Wan et al. 2008, open primary article. The PMC record identifies the authors, title, journal year, and DOI; this supports the established source-ambiguity boundary.

## Result for B0

This screen narrows the claim boundary and rejects “forward-model ensemble” as a novelty argument by itself. The 2026 Localize-MI study further narrows the empirical space for a distinct contribution. Together with the pinned base, license, equation trace, Track A parity scope, and comparison matrix, it supports **B0 PASS as a bounded base-selection/prior-art decision**. The route is a GBFs reproduction plus inspectable source-distinguishability software; no new-method claim is accepted. Independent technical challenge remains necessary before any future distinct-method claim and for the final B8 outside check. B0 does not require a researcher decision-value result to choose this bounded software route; B7 determines whether the tool adds practical value beyond standard diagnostics.
