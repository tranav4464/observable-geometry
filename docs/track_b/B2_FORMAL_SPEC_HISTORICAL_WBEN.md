## Purpose

It must say what the model receives, what measured quantity it predicts or assesses, what comparison will decide whether the build adds value, and what the result cannot establish. The task must be feasible with the released data and distinct from the closest prior work.

The on004752 cohort was already used by the WBEN paper, so this work is a reproduction and challenge on the released cohort, not an independent-cohort replication.

## Candidate mathematical contribution, not yet accepted

The broad framing of separating predictability from identifiability in scalp-to-iEEG inference is already described in a [2026 review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26998). Profile likelihood is established for partially observed dynamical models ([Raue et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp358)), has been applied to effective-connectivity parameters in DCM ([Arand et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2015.00043)), and neural-population parameter identifiability from EEG has been analyzed directly ([Hartoyo et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006694)). Therefore a WBEN edge-wise profile is not currently a defensible new-method claim; at most it is a candidate application or diagnostic. Keep it only if an exact additional algorithmic distinction survives equation-level comparison and its output changes a useful, prespecified decision. Otherwise drop the novelty claim. The focused audit is recorded in `outputs/track_b/20261022T194717Z_b2_neural_identifiability_prior_audit/SESSION.md`.

Cross-load prediction of measured iEEG remains a candidate **empirical challenge**, not a new method. Direct scalp-to-iEEG feature and waveform prediction are established, and cross-task EEG decoding is established in adjacent settings. A newly audited 2026 preprint by Dong et al. directly claims scalp-to-iEEG reconstruction on the same `ds004752` release and scalp-only generation at inference. It reports an 8:2 non-overlapping train/test split, but the inspected methods do not say whether that split is by participant, session, trial, or window and do not describe a completely held-out working-memory set size. The preprint labels the dataset visual categorization, conflicting with the official NEMAR description of a verbal working-memory task; the supplement says 15 participants while the methods exclude sub-10 and report 14. Therefore it preempts generic reconstruction claims but does not settle the precise held-load endpoint. Its results remain unverified preprint claims. See `outputs/track_b/20261022T211614Z_b2_dong_ds004752_overlap_audit/SESSION.md`.

Same-task paired scalp/iEEG load effects and cross-modal coupling are also established. Boran et al. report load-sensitive hippocampal firing and scalp/hippocampal coupling over set sizes 4/6/8, while Ye et al. analyze load- and phase-dependent coupling between low-frequency scalp phase and intracranial high-gamma amplitude. These studies preempt broad claims about discovering load-sensitive coupling or estimating any intracranial feature from scalp EEG. The inspected sources do not establish the narrower target of scalp-only iEEG waveform prediction at a fully held-out set size. This distinction leaves a possible replication/challenge question, not an algorithm-novelty claim. See `outputs/track_b/20261023T051500Z_b2_working_memory_coupling_audit/SESSION.md`.

Abdi-Sargezeh et al. (2025) also report scalp-to-foramen-ovale-iEEG translation in 18 epilepsy patients, including inter-subject evaluation, with the main downstream endpoint being IED classification rather than general contact-level waveform fidelity. Its data and code are available from the authors on request, not through a verified public repository. This further preempts generic cross-subject translation as a novelty claim but does not test the held-load target.

The current source screen has not identified a direct evaluation of paired scalp-to-iEEG contact prediction where an entire working-memory set size is absent from training and calibration. This is a bounded search result, not proof of priority or novelty. If it survives the remaining task-level review, its contribution would be a reproducible real-data challenge and software result, not a novel algorithm. The protocol must compare against direct regression and the strongest usable reconstruction prior, and must keep the target as measured iEEG under the recorded reference. Do not call generated estimates recovered latent brain activity. The profile/identifiability candidate remains optional established analysis and is not presently the contribution.

## Feasible task boundary from B1

- Dataset: NEMAR `on004752` v1.0.0, CC0; use only the pinned released paired recordings.
- Representation: paired event-relative epochs of eight seconds, mapped from one-based inclusive event samples to zero-based time and resampled to the common 50 Hz grid.
- Session inventory: 68 paired sessions in the release versus 71 reported by the source paper; the difference is unresolved. Two pairs with malformed absolute EDF timestamps are excluded by the predeclared metadata-only rule, leaving at most 66 pairs.
- Exposure: participants sub-01 through sub-03 are development-only. Keep event rows and outcomes for sub-04 through sub-15 sealed until B5 protocol lock and preflight.
- Claim limits: B1 does not establish continuous-stream synchronization, sub-sample timing, causal connectivity, complete spatial truth, or ground truth for unobserved regions and edges.

The 50 Hz event-relative representation establishes feasibility only. The precise preprocessing used for the final comparison, including reference, filtering, resampling implementation, artifact handling, and units, must be documented and frozen in B2/B5.

## Candidate estimand, not yet accepted

The current candidate is prediction of **measured iEEG at prespecified held-out contacts or windows** from a declared set of scalp EEG and, if justified, permitted observed iEEG measurements. The target is the recorded signal under the specified task and acquisition protocol. It is not the complete latent brain state, all neural connections, causal influence, or the activity of unrecorded tissue.

The original provisional split jointly held out working-memory load and session to control session leakage. A development-only count audit shows that it is a poor primary design: across 42 participant/session/load folds, the minimum artifact-unflagged test size is 7 trials and the minimum training size is 53. It also mixes load transfer with session transfer, so a failure cannot be attributed to either source of shift. See `outputs/track_b/20261022T202100Z_b2_joint_fold_feasibility/`.

The better-separated candidate is a **within-participant held-load test**: for each participant and each load `k ∈ {4,6,8}`, train on the other two loads across all sessions and test only the held load across all sessions. In the three development participants, all nine folds are nonempty; artifact-unflagged test counts range from 44 to 126 (median 61), and training counts range from 88 to 223 (median 115). Training and test share session identities but not trials or load labels. This estimates transfer to an unseen set size within sessions represented during training; it does **not** test session or participant transfer. A separate held-session arm trains on all loads in all other sessions and tests the held session; its 14 development folds are also nonempty (test minimum 39, median 46; training minimum 84, median 271), but it does not test held-load transfer. Do not combine these two estimands into one primary score. The alternative counts are in `outputs/track_b/20261022T202200Z_b2_split_alternatives/`.

The earlier five-fold whole-trial split was feasible but is no longer the preferred tuning split for the held-load task: each validation fold contained both outer-training loads, so it mainly assessed interpolation among observed loads rather than transfer to a new load. A development-metadata audit now supports **inner leave-one-source-load-out validation**. For each outer held load `k`, the other two loads form two inner directions: fit on one complete load across all sessions and validate on the other, then reverse the direction. Across 3 development participants × 3 outer held loads × 2 inner directions, all 18 folds are nonempty; the minimum all-trial fit and validation sizes are 46, and the minimum `Artifact==0` descriptive counts are 44. For tuning, compute each contact's NRMSE using the SD from that inner fit set only, macro-average equally across contacts, then average the two validation-load scores equally. This better matches load-transfer selection while leaving the outer held load unused. It does not test session transfer, establish power, or prove model adequacy. Refit all preprocessing and model parameters inside each fold and use identical inner assignments for every method. The earlier random-trial feasibility audit remains historical evidence, superseded for tuning by `outputs/track_b/20261022T205629Z_b2_inner_load_cv_audit/SESSION.md` and `inner_load_holdout_counts.json`.

NLGC provides a relevant but non-interchangeable regularization precedent: its published method uses K-fold validation to choose a shared L1 penalty through an estimation-stability criterion, selecting the candidate with lowest cross-fold variance in reconstructed MEG estimates, then reuses that penalty for reduced models ([Soleimani et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9435442/)). That criterion targets stability of a source-network estimate, whereas the current candidate task targets held-load prediction of measured iEEG waveforms. Do not describe the NLGC score as equivalent to predictive error or as a new contribution. If the held-load prediction task survives B2, choose regularization and effective observation-covariance settings only from whole-trial inner folds using a prespecified, target-aligned validation score; retain stability as a secondary diagnostic only if it changes a defined decision. This remains provisional until the model interface and exact score are independently reviewed. Source-access limits and the scope of this comparison are recorded in `outputs/track_b/20261022T205417Z_b2_nlgc_regularization_audit/SESSION.md`.

These are metadata feasibility counts, not evidence of statistical power. They use `Artifact==0` only for descriptive counting; the artifact rule still needs a prespecified signal-quality protocol. Incorrect-response trials remain eligible for the candidate signal-prediction task because correctness is not the target. The held-load split is the preferred candidate for the primary challenge, with a held-session analysis reported separately only if its role and metric are predeclared. This remains provisional until the WBEN/contact target interface, direct-prior comparison, preprocessing, baseline suite, primary metric, and uncertainty method are finalized.

B2 must choose exactly one primary task formulation. It must distinguish among:

- held-out trials or windows at contacts available during training;
- held-out contacts or regions within a participant;
- transfer to participants whose data were not used for fitting;
- prediction of a future time interval versus reconstruction/imputation of a contemporaneous held-out measurement.

These tasks answer different questions and cannot be blended into one score. If the data do not support a credible split for the intended claim, narrow the claim or stop the proposed endpoint.

#### Candidate temporal window: final two seconds of maintenance

The task window is now mapped directly by the WBEN paper itself. Appendix A.4 says the released 8-second epoch is fixation [0,1), encoding [1,3), maintenance [3,6), and response [6,8); it says the analysis uses the final 2 seconds of maintenance and downsamples both modalities to 50 Hz ([Yang & Liu, NeurIPS 2025, Appendix A.4](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/82a6afb1f666daaeec8da4d775452bc7-Paper-Conference.pdf)). The original dataset paper independently defines a 2-second encoding period followed by 3-second maintenance before probe, and reports spectra over the last 2 seconds of maintenance ([Boran et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6972902/), Methods and Fig. 3). The aligned NEMAR companion also places probe onset at epoch onset +6 seconds ([NEMAR companion dataset](https://nemar.org/dataset/nm000363)).

For the candidate cross-load endpoint, use the **half-open interval [4.0, 6.0) seconds relative to the corrected zero-based start of each released 8-second epoch**. On the WBEN common 50 Hz grid this is sample indices `[200:300]`, 100 samples per trial; it excludes the probe at +6 seconds and the post-probe response. The source dataset and WBEN describe this phase mapping directly; the local BIDS event table establishes the released 8-second epoch bounds. A metadata-only check of 14 already-authorized development event pairs is recorded in `outputs/track_b/20261022T204000Z_b2_maintenance_window_mapping/`.

This is a **candidate task-window choice**, not yet a locked preprocessing contract or evidence of prediction benefit. WBEN's published real-data analysis estimates phase-specific networks from task data and does not demonstrate scalp-only prediction of referenced contact waveforms at a held-out load. The paper names 50 Hz downsampling but does not specify the digital anti-alias filter or resampling implementation; our fixed-header audit also found blank EDF prefilter fields, while acquisition/cutoff metadata conflict. Do not describe an implementation as a faithful preprocessing reproduction until this is reconciled. Keep WBEN's network-analysis reproduction distinct from the proposed maintenance-window, scalp-only measured-contact prediction challenge. Before B2 closes, settle acquisition/software filtering, trial/channel quality rules, the exact state reset at trial boundaries, training-only normalization, fair direct-regression baseline, primary score, and participant-level uncertainty. Never concatenate trials as one continuous state trajectory.

#### Independent trial sequences and model order

The held-load candidate contains 100 samples per trial after mapping the final two maintenance seconds to 50 Hz. Each trial is an independent sequence: initialize the latent state from the same prespecified initial distribution for every trial, sum trial log likelihoods, and compute transition sufficient statistics only within a trial. For `N` trials of length `T=100` and a VAR order `K=2`, there are `N(T-K)=98N` valid lagged transitions; no transition may cross a trial boundary. At confirmation inference, initialize each test trial independently, condition only on that trial's scalp EEG, and predict its contemporaneous recorded iEEG under the declared common reference. This is same-time reconstruction of a measured target, not future forecasting, source recovery, or zero-shot decoding.

This order choice follows the WBEN real-data analysis, which Appendix A.4 says used a second-order VAR. The current `fit_wben_k1` implementation is first-order and accepts one contiguous sequence; applying it to concatenated task trials would create false cross-trial transitions. Thus it is not directly suitable for the candidate evaluation. Before model fitting, the software must expose a multi-sequence, order-2 fit or an equivalent mathematically verified implementation, with per-trial initial-state handling and trial-summed EM sufficient statistics. This is an implementation requirement, not a new contribution. If the second-order contact model proves unidentifiable or numerically unstable on real development data, document the failure and compare a justified lower-order adaptation without calling it faithful WBEN reproduction.

#### Candidate transparent preprocessing and primary score

The paper gives the target sample rate but not a reproducible digital filter sequence. A transparent project-specific candidate is to resample each complete 8-second epoch before cropping, using the pinned SciPy `1.18.1` `scipy.signal.resample_poly` default symmetric Kaiser FIR (`window=('kaiser', 5.0)`, `padtype='constant'`, zero-phase): EEG `up=1, down=4`; iEEG `up=1, down=40`. This supplies a low-pass anti-alias filter at the 50 Hz target. Crop `[200:300]` only after resampling, keeping the analysis window far from epoch boundaries. Add no separate high-pass or notch filter unless a documented source requirement changes this decision. This is a declared reproducible adaptation, not a claim to match the authors' undisclosed resampling filter. Preserve the acquisition filter uncertainty and the BIDS sidecar/channel-table conflict in every result. The SciPy operation and boundary behavior are documented in the [official `resample_poly` reference](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.resample_poly.html).

Candidate trial handling: exclude trials whose released `Artifact` flag is 1; retain stable participant-specific channels because no channel `status` flags exist; exclude a target/input channel only if training data contain a non-finite value or exactly zero standard deviation, and apply that same training-derived channel mask to all methods. Before normalization, the data loader must apply the `channels.tsv` unit declaration: development EDF physical-dimension fields are blank while BIDS channel tables declare `μV`. MNE's `read_raw_edf(..., units="uV")` is the documented override for missing EDF units; the real-data v3 audit checked the header, channel order, and units for all 14 development pairs. Standardize each EEG input channel and iEEG target contact using means and standard deviations from training trials only. If a test target contains non-finite values, mask that contact-time pair identically for every method and report the coverage; never impute a test target or use it for filtering. See `outputs/track_b/20261023T231500Z_b2_observed_signal_scale_audit_v3/SESSION.md`; the erroneous first-pass conversion is retained and invalidated at `outputs/track_b/20261023T221500Z_b2_observed_signal_scale_audit/SESSION.md`.

A real-data quality check applied this candidate resampling/cropping path to 629 `Artifact==0` development trials across 14 paired sessions. It found zero non-finite and zero exactly constant channel-trial windows among 41,192 channel-trial traces in `[4,6)` s (6,968 EEG and 34,224 iEEG). This supports no additional data-derived exclusion for those development observations, but does not establish that the recordings are physiologically artifact-free, uncropped, or correctly acquisition-filtered. No amplitude-based threshold is inferred because per-participant and modality scales differ. See `outputs/track_b/20261022T205915Z_b2_real_signal_quality_audit/SESSION.md` and `signal_window_quality.json`.

#### Observation-noise status: unresolved for the adaptation

WBEN Section 2.1 treats EEG and iEEG observation covariances `P` and `S` as known and estimates only the state dynamics `Phi` and process covariance `Q`. Appendix A.4 gives zero-mean Gaussian noise standard deviations of 1 for EEG and 0.1 for iEEG when empty-room recordings are unavailable. It does not document signal normalization or a physical-unit mapping for those values. They therefore cannot be transferred as calibrated contact-level noise covariances for this dataset. Our contact-state model also replaces WBEN's physical regional observation maps with a learned map, so its observation-noise treatment is an explicit adaptation choice, not a faithful reproduction. Before B2 passes, specify the covariance structure, units, training-only estimate or inner-validation selection rule, and how latent-state/noise ambiguity is controlled. Do not fit noise scales using held-load targets or confirmation outcomes. The source-level audit is `outputs/track_b/20261023T220000Z_b2_wben_observation_noise_audit/SESSION.md`.

A real development-only scale audit found participant-dependent EEG/iEEG signal variability and confirmed the dataset's unit crosswalk, but it cannot estimate sensor noise: the fixation interval is brain activity, not an empty-room recording. For the candidate prediction adaptation, observation covariances should therefore be described as **effective model-error covariances on train-standardized observations**, not physical sensor-noise estimates. A provisional candidate is isotropic `P = α I` and `S = β I` on the standardized EEG and iEEG spaces, with `(α, β)` selected only by nested inner leave-one-source-load-out validation inside each outer training split. This is a modeling proposal, not yet a locked choice; B2 remains open until the exact grid, fit stability rule, and parameter-identifiability limits are specified and independently reviewed. Do not use fixation variability as a proxy for instrument noise. The scale evidence is in `outputs/track_b/20261023T231500Z_b2_observed_signal_scale_audit_v3/SESSION.md`.

Use normalized RMSE so contacts with larger numerical scale do not dominate. For participant `p`, held load `k`, contact `c`, method `m`, define `NRMSE[p,k,c,m] = sqrt(mean_test((z - zhat)^2)) / sd_train[p,c]`. Average contacts equally within each fold and the three load folds equally within participant; the participant is the uncertainty unit. The primary paired contrast is `Delta[p] = NRMSE_direct-ridge[p] - NRMSE_state-space[p]`, so positive values favor the state-space model. Also report the training-mean predictor and require the state-space method to outperform it before claiming useful prediction. Report every participant's value and a 95% participant-level bootstrap interval for the mean paired contrast; do not treat windows, time samples, contacts, sessions, or loads as independent subjects. This score is an evaluation metric, not a new method. The inner-validation split, hyperparameter grids/budget, exact noise parameterization, missing-test-target policy, practical effect threshold, and independent review still require protocol review before B2 passes.

### Provisional contact-state model interface

The atlas-free fallback is a clearly named **WBEN-inspired contact-state adaptation**, not a faithful replication of WBEN's 69 Harvard–Oxford region model. For a participant with `M` stable recorded SEEG contacts, let `x_t ∈ R^M` be the latent contact-indexed state, `z_t = I_M x_t + e_t` the paired iEEG observation during training, and `y_t = G x_t + v_t` the scalp EEG observation. The contact-level dynamics follow the WBEN VAR/state-space form `x_t = Σ_{j=1}^K Φ_j x_{t-j} + w_t`. Fit the cross-modal map and dynamics using paired training trials only. The observation-noise covariance rule remains an explicit B2 blocker; do not silently inherit WBEN's paper values or fit covariance parameters until the units, training-only rule, and identifiability safeguards are defined. At held-load inference, supply scalp EEG `y` only and output the posterior predictive mean and covariance for the recorded, common-reference contact signal `z`; do not use held-load iEEG in filtering, normalization, feature selection, or model choice. `G` is a data-estimated cross-modal observation map, not a physical lead field. No atlas, anatomical localization, whole-brain network, or reference-independent-voltage claim follows from this adaptation.

Compare this state-space adaptation with (1) a direct regularized scalp-to-iEEG predictor using the same training trials, target contacts, preprocessing and tuning budget, and (2) a training-mean predictor. A held-load within-load-trial split is a secondary in-distribution reference only. Use the NRMSE and participant-level paired contrast defined above as the single primary metric; do not use a second, differently normalized “skill” definition. Report all three held loads separately as well as the prespecified participant summary. Failure to beat the training-mean predictor falsifies predictive usefulness; failure to beat matched direct regression rejects an added-value claim for the state-space adaptation. Exact preprocessing, artifact/channel exclusions, uncertainty procedure, and a minimally useful effect threshold must be fixed before the confirmation cohort is opened. Training on paired iEEG from the same participant at other loads means this is a calibrated scientific benchmark, not a non-invasive zero-shot decoder.

### Task-level prior-art finding

WBEN's primary real-data result analyzes inferred network patterns during encoding and maintenance; its paper reports known-truth synthetic graph-recovery experiments, which are prior literature and are not permitted active work here ([official NeurIPS record](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/82a6afb1f666daaeec8da4d775452bc7-Abstract-Conference.html)). Subramanian et al. predict scalp-to-iEEG spectral power in 20 participants and report leave-one-patient-out regional prediction; the target is spectral features, not a held-out cognitive-load condition ([full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12026988/)). NeuroFlowNet and CAST occupy within-participant and calibrated cross-participant waveform reconstruction, respectively; their exact data/task comparisons remain in the method matrix. A 2026 review emphasizes that these prediction targets are distinct from identifiability and fidelity ([review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26998)). The candidate cross-load question may therefore be a useful empirical challenge, but neither the task nor its standard metrics constitute a new mathematical method. This audit is not an exhaustive systematic review.

#### Updated task/base compatibility audit (22 October 2026)

The direct-reconstruction screen has been expanded to include the NeuroFlowNet conditional-flow paper/repository, CAST, a 2026 patient-specific IED localization-plus-waveform preprint, and 2025 working-memory load analyses. These establish that generic within-person waveform generation, calibrated cross-person waveform prediction, temporal event localization, and load decoding are occupied neighboring tasks. CAST's manuscript calls ds004752 a visual-categorization cohort, whereas the official NEMAR/OpenNeuro record describes a verbal working-memory Sternberg task. Its exact task labels and analyzed subset therefore require reconciliation before it can serve as a matched baseline. NeuroFlowNet links an author repository with implementation files but no visible repository license; no code reuse is authorized by the current evidence.

There is also a model-target mismatch that the earlier candidate sketch did not make explicit: WBEN is a joint scalp+iEEG state-space method whose reported real-data output is network-flow estimates during encoding and maintenance. It is not the paper's demonstrated scalp-only-to-iEEG waveform predictor. An algebraic derivation now shows a possible inference adaptation: fit all parameters on training trials with both modalities, then run a scalp-only Kalman filter on each held-out trial and predict its iEEG target as `H_z m_{t|t}` with covariance `H_z P_{t|t} H_zᵀ + R_z`; no test-trial iEEG enters the filter. This establishes mathematical feasibility, not a published WBEN result, implementation, empirical prediction, or novel method. See `outputs/track_b/20261022T200010Z_b2_wben_scalp_only_inference_derivation/SESSION.md`. A direct scalp-to-iEEG regressor remains a separate comparator. The candidate is now a testable adaptation only if the target operator and train/test map are fixed and supported.

### Provisional cross-load protocol sketch (not locked)

For each participant and each set size `k ∈ {4, 6, 8}`, fit on that participant's trials at the other two set sizes and evaluate on all eligible trials at `k`. ### Load-conditioned state-space extension: novelty rejected

The simple extension `A(k) = A0 + k A1`, or a smooth learned map from working-memory load `k` to state-space parameters, is not a new mathematical method. Conditionally Linear Dynamical Systems already model latent transitions as `x[t+1] = A(u[t]) x[t] + b(u[t]) + noise` with a learned, potentially nonlinear mapping from observed experimental conditions `u` to dynamics. Load-dependent effective connectivity and load decoding are also established. The same-task iEEG study by Yang et al. reports power-based decoding for set sizes 4, 6, and 8 and load-related synchronization. These works do not directly establish scalp-only prediction of measured iEEG at a fully withheld load, but they rule out load modulation itself as the contribution. Treat a load-conditioned state-space model as a comparator or practical modeling option only; it must earn retention by a prespecified held-out real-data benefit. See `outputs/track_b/20261022T201900Z_b2_load_modulated_state_space_prior_audit/SESSION.md` and the primary sources linked there.

A possible parameterization is `Phi_{k,s} = Phi_{k,shared} + Delta_{k,s}`, where `s` is set size. A shrinkage or group penalty on `Delta` could trade shared dynamics against load-specific deviations. This is a conventional multitask structure until proven otherwise. It should be implemented only if prior-art review identifies a precise gap and the real-data task shows value beyond pooled and condition-specific alternatives. The model comparison must prevent the target iEEG contact from entering either training observations or test-time inputs.

### Proposed operational contract v2 for independent challenge (not locked)

The following candidate choices make the task reviewable. They supersede earlier vague references to a same-time-only ridge comparator and to fitting separate iEEG sensor noise, but remain provisional until independent review and the remaining prior-art review are complete.

**Question and estimand.** For each eligible participant and held load `k∈{4,6,8}`, train on the other two loads using all eligible sessions. At test time, give the model only the eight scalp channels shared by all three development participants (`A1,A2,C3,C4,F3,F4,O1,O2`) from each held-load trial, and predict the concurrently recorded iEEG contact vector during `[4.0,6.0)` seconds. The target is the dataset-declared common-reference waveform in microvolts. The model is trained on paired data from the same participant at other loads, so this is a calibrated within-person load-transfer benchmark, not a zero-shot or thought-decoding BCI. It does not test new-session transfer because sessions occur in both train and test.

**Preprocessing and exclusions.** Load EDF data with the channel-table microvolt units explicitly passed to MNE. Resample each complete 8-second epoch with SciPy `resample_poly` at 200→50 Hz for EEG and 2,000→50 Hz for iEEG, using the documented default Kaiser FIR and constant boundary extension; then crop sample indices `[200:300]`. Add no project-specific high-pass, notch, or band-pass filter. This is a transparent project choice, not verified reproduction of the acquisition or WBEN filter. Exclude only trials with the dataset artifact flag. Include response-incorrect trials because the outcome is signal prediction, not behavioral accuracy; report a correctness-stratified sensitivity analysis if sample counts permit. Determine nonfinite or zero-variance channel exclusions from each training fold only and apply the identical mask to every method. Standardize each channel with training-fold means and scales only; report contact predictions in microvolts and normalized errors.

**Candidate state-space predictor.** Define the state as the recorded target `x_t:=z_t` during training. Fit a VAR(2) with ridge-regularized least squares to within-trial target sequences; no lag crosses trial boundaries. Fit `G` in `y_t=Gx_t+v_t` by ridge regression on the same paired training samples. Estimate effective `Q` from VAR residuals and `R_y` from scalp-map residuals with `sklearn.covariance.ledoit_wolf` from scikit-learn 1.9.1, `assume_centered=False`, and `block_size=1000`, fit only on training residuals. These covariances describe residual structure, not physical sensor noise. The Track B CPU dependency is pinned in `requirements/track-b-cpu.txt`; this does not modify the sealed foundation environment. Estimate the joint initial distribution of `(x_1,x_0)` from the first two standardized target samples of training trials. At test, initialize a fresh filter from that training-only distribution; update `x_0` with `y_0`, update the joint state with `y_1`, then apply the VAR(2) transition and scalp observation updates for later samples. Use posterior mean for prediction; retain covariance only as model-conditional uncertainty, not calibrated source uncertainty.

For the filter, use companion state `s_t=[x_t;x_{t-1}]`, transition `F=[[A_1,A_2],[I,0]]`, scalp observation `H=[G,0]`, and process covariance `Q_s=[[Q,0],[0,0]]`. `Q_s` is positive semidefinite and singular by construction. The new adaptation therefore needs an implementation that accepts semidefinite process covariance and maintains a positive-definite innovation covariance through the shrunk `R_y`; the existing `fit_wben_k1` positive-definite-only validation is not reusable as-is. Keep the adaptation code in a separate module so it cannot silently alter the paper-faithful WBEN implementation.

**Matched baselines and tuning.** Compare the state-space predictor with (1) training-mean contact prediction and (2) a causal finite-impulse-response ridge predictor from the current and past scalp samples to the contact vector. Left-pad unavailable FIR history at a trial start with zero in standardized coordinates, equivalent to the training mean. Tune the state VAR ridge and scalp-map ridge over the same five-value log grid `10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2,10^4` (25 joint configurations). Tune FIR history length over `{1,5,10,25,50}` samples and its ridge penalty over that same five-value grid (25 configurations). For each outer held load, use two inner leave-one-source-load-out directions across all sessions. Refit scaling, masks, covariance estimates, and model parameters inside every inner fold; choose configuration by equal-weight mean of contact-wise training-SD-normalized RMSE across both validation loads. Keep the outer held load entirely unused in tuning.

**Primary score and uncertainty.** For participant `p`, outer held load `k`, and contact `c`, calculate `RMSE_{p,k,c}` across all eligible held-load trials and the 100 time points, then divide by the SD of contact `c` computed from outer-training trials only. Average equally across contacts to obtain `S_{p,k}`; average equally across the three held loads for participant score `S_{p,m}` for method `m`. The primary paired benefit is `Δ_p=S_{p,FIR}-S_{p,state}`, where positive values favor the state-space model. Report every participant's score and a 95% percentile bootstrap interval over participants (10,000 resamples, fixed seed `20261023` recorded in the run manifest); do not treat contacts, samples, trials, or held-load folds as independent subjects. Also report each held load separately and the training-mean results. A predictive-usefulness claim requires lower error than the training mean; an added-value claim requires positive participant-level benefit over the causal FIR baseline with its 95% interval above zero. This does not by itself establish practical utility, which is reserved for B7.

**Participant/contact eligibility.** Apply the B1 parser rule before trial selection, excluding only `sub-06/ses-04` and `sub-14/ses-06`. For each participant, define target contacts as the intersection of iEEG channel labels across the remaining paired sessions; this is a metadata-only correspondence rule, not anatomical validation. Require all eight prespecified scalp channels in each included paired session. Include a participant only if, after excluding dataset-flagged artifact trials, at least 30 trials remain at each set size 4, 6, and 8 across eligible sessions. The 30-trial threshold is fixed below the development-only minimum of 44 artifact-unflagged held-load trials observed across the nine development folds; it is not selected from confirmation outcomes.
