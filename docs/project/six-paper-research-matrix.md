# Six primary papers for the source-validation article

**Research question:** How can a researcher tell whether a brain map identifies a neural source rather than merely producing a plausible answer?

**Selection rule:** These six papers test different links in a validation chain. Two use imposed, spatially known electrical inputs but explicitly limit transfer to spontaneous neural activity; one uses simultaneous endogenous scalp and intracranial recordings; one uses clinical outcome; one quantifies inverse-method resolution under declared source/noise assumptions; and one tests head-conductivity uncertainty. A high-profile inverse result alone would duplicate the Learn article's GBF discussion and would not answer this validation question as directly. Each source below is a primary study or primary dataset/analysis paper, not a review.

| Study and full primary source | Measurement, sample and question | Method and independent comparison | Main result and relevant figure | Limit on what it establishes | Role in synthesis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Mikulan et al., *Scientific Data* (2020)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-020-0467-x.pdf), DOI 10.1038/s41597-020-0467-x | Localize-MI: **7 patients**, **61 stimulation sessions**, implanted contact locations, participant MRI and up to **256 scalp EEG channels**. Can a known intracranial injection site make source-imaging pipelines testable? | Biphasic pulses were delivered through adjacent implanted contacts while HD-EEG recorded the resulting scalp artifact. The paper tried MNE, dSPM and eLORETA, multiple regularizations and channel montages against contact locations. | Technical validation, **Fig. 3**: with each session's selected optimal configuration, mean minimum distance was **5.39 mm** (SD 2.61); across all parameter configurations, distances spread to roughly **50 mm**. The apparent accuracy depends on method and tuning. **Fig. 2** shows site and MRI coverage. | The measured event is an **imposed electrical artifact**, not a spontaneous neural current. Sites cluster within patients; choosing the best configuration per session is not held-out accuracy. The dataset paper itself cautions physiological generalization. | A spatially known input is powerful for debugging geometry and algorithms, but only validates the imposed-input task. This is also the dataset from which Observable Geometry's one-model example derives. |
| [Unnwongse et al., *Brain Communications* (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9942548/), DOI 10.1093/braincomms/fcad023 | **11 enrolled**, **9 with analyzable stimulation potentials**, **37 scalp EEG channels**. How do source depth and skull conductivity affect localization against known stimulation contacts? | Individual four-compartment FEM heads, CT-to-MRI contact localization, averaged bipolar stimulation potentials and dipole fitting under **11 skull-conductivity values**. **341 contact-pair averages** generated **3,619 accepted dipole fits** after model variations; 3,619 is not 3,619 independent patients or sites. | Abstract and **Figs. 3–4 / Table 1**: mean errors **10.3–26 mm** across depth and conductivity conditions; deeper sources and lower assumed skull conductivity worsened mean error. **Fig. 1** documents the registration and model workflow. | Pulse potentials have high SNR and artificial origin; the study explicitly calls for testing whether conductivity findings transfer to true neural signals. Contact location is known, but the fitted dipole is a model of the injected field. | A known endpoint still leaves material forward-model uncertainty. An error number without the conductivity and depth conditions is incomplete. |
| [Zauli et al., *Journal of Neuroscience Methods* (2024)](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/e5cb014e-6527-4977-8590-6d80f9f288e3/1-s2.0-S0165027024001389-main.pdf), DOI 10.1016/j.jneumeth.2024.110193 | **8 patients** selected from 50, with simultaneous **256-channel scalp EEG and SEEG**; four hippocampal IED cases and four focal-cortical-dysplasia cases. Can SEEG-timed endogenous epileptic discharges hidden in single scalp trials be localized? | Synchronized systems, SEEG-defined IED timings, scalp event averaging, MRI/electrode registration, 3-layer BEM, and dSPM/eLORETA over several regularization settings. Distance was measured from the inverse peak to the SEEG contact identifying the IED. | **Fig. 2** shows events obscure in single scalp trials but emerging in SEEG-aligned averages. Mean **82.5 IEDs per patient**; **9.4%** of SEEG spikes were detectable on scalp alone on average. **Table 2 / Fig. 3:** mean best-per-patient distance **19.20 mm** versus **35.56 mm** averaged across methods/parameters. | An implanted contact samples one location, not every active source. The best-parameter result is optimistic relative to a fixed, prospective choice. One centre, eight patients and short recordings limit generalization. SEEG timing is unavailable in a routine scalp-only deployment. | This crosses from artifact to a real neural event, while exposing how averaging, sparse intracranial sampling and parameter choice affect the meaning of “validated.” |
| [Hauk et al., *NeuroImage* (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7793168/), DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117430 | **5 healthy participants** supplied resting EEG/MEG noise and anatomy. How do inverse methods spread or misplace known *constructed* cortical patches at different signal-to-noise ratios? | Individually uniform activation of **500 cortical patches per hemisphere** was projected through the forward model and added to measured resting recordings. An empirical resolution matrix extended the linear **R = KG** idea to nonlinear inverse methods. MNE, dSPM, sLORETA, eLORETA and MxNE were compared using peak error, spatial dispersion and ROC/precision-recall measures. | **Figs. 4–7:** methods differed in leakage, spread and localization; performance varied with SNR and source location. The paper highlights that point-source, infinite-SNR properties do not predict all finite-SNR patch results. **Fig. 1** defines patches, **Fig. 2** the empirical matrix. | The activity patches and their ground truth were **constructed**, even though the noise was recorded. The five-person hardware/montage and chosen patch family do not validate recovery of unknown endogenous events. | Shows what a model-conditional resolution test can measure, and why a good-looking peak alone is an incomplete performance measure. It is close prior art for the Build's PSF comparison. |
| [Vorwerk et al., *Frontiers in Neuroscience* (2019)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2019.00531/full), DOI 10.3389/fnins.2019.00531 | One measured somatosensory evoked-potential case (**972 recorded events** before cleaning) with individual MRI and digitized scalp electrodes. How much can uncertain tissue conductivity move a fitted EEG source? | A finite-element head model and generalized polynomial chaos approximation varied skin, skull, CSF, gray-matter and white-matter conductivities. The authors checked surrogate accuracy and generated many conductivity-specific forward models; the observed P20/N20 response was fitted repeatedly. | **Figs. 4, 7–10:** assumed skin and skull conductivity strongly changed recovered source depth and location; gray/white matter chiefly affected orientation in this scenario; CSF variation had little effect within its tested range. **Fig. 1** shows the measured response, **Table 1** the conductivity intervals. | This is a sensitivity study of one SEP/single-dipole fitting scenario, not proof of the true generator's location. Results depend on the assumed conductivity ranges, model and registration. The paper warns against treating its optimal values as universal. | A map can change because the forward physics changed while the measured EEG did not. A source claim needs model-sensitivity analysis, not just inverse-solver comparison. |
| [Vorderwülbecke et al., *Clinical Neurophysiology* (2020)](https://cibm.ch/wp-content/uploads/1-s2.0-S1388245720304934-main.pdf), DOI 10.1016/j.clinph.2020.09.018 | **45 surgical patients**, presurgical **257-channel EEG acquisition**, individual MRI and **12-month outcome**; **30** had favourable outcomes. Can clinical outcome test whether interictal source maxima relate to treated tissue? | The authors averaged IEDs, compared **257, 219, 204 and 156 channel** analyses, and tested source timing across the rising phase. Estimated maxima were compared with the resection and outcome. **Fig. 1** defines montages and timing; **Figs. 2–4 / Table 1** show outcome by setup, timing and anatomy. | With **204 channels** and favourable outcome, maxima lay **<10 mm from resection in 67%** and in the affected sublobe in **83%**. Source imaging at **50% of the IED rising phase** performed best in this study. | Resection and seizure outcome are **indirect, spatially coarse** evidence for a neural generator. The authors caution that poor outcome cannot be read as proof of a wrong map, because surgery can fail for other reasons. No simultaneous intracranial reference for the events is provided. | Adds a clinically meaningful endpoint while separating useful treatment concordance from exact physiological source identification. |

## Cross-paper comparison to preserve in Article 2

1. **What is known independently?** Mikulan and Unnwongse know the stimulation contacts but measure a pulse artifact. Zauli records real IEDs simultaneously inside and outside the head but sees only implanted regions. The surgical-outcome study has a clinically meaningful endpoint whose spatial specificity is limited. Hauk knows constructed sources exactly but does not test endogenous recovery. Vorwerk exposes uncertainty in the forward mapping itself.
2. **What can make a result look stronger than it is?** Best-per-case tuning (Mikulan and Zauli), repeated fits counted as though they were independent observations (Unnwongse), assumptions about constructed patches (Hauk), unverified conductivity (Vorwerk), and treating a broad resection as a point reference (outcome study).
3. **What would be a stronger chain?** Independently checked electrode-to-MRI geometry, prospective model and parameter selection, a realistic uncertainty range, simultaneous measured neural timing where possible, a matched existing-method baseline, and an endpoint whose granularity matches the anatomical claim. No single study here supplies all of those at once.

## Source and reading audit

Full primary text was checked for all six: open article/XML for Unnwongse and Hauk; the university-hosted author PDF for Zauli; the publisher PDFs for Mikulan and Vorwerk; and the open original 2020 surgical-outcome study for Vorderwülbecke. Methods, results, figure captions and limitation sections informed the matrix. Citations above link to the actual full text. The Zauli PDF was accessible to the web reader but returned HTTP 403 to local command-line download; its full PDF text, including figures and tables, was inspected through the source link.
